# CV Professional Summary

The Professional Summary is a short snapshot at the top of the CV. It tells a recruiter who the candidate is, what they do best and what they bring to the role. Recruiters skim, so the summary has one job: **sell the candidate and make the reader keep going.**

Candidates are senior professionals, mostly in data science and IT, placed by a data science consultancy. Write for a hiring manager who has a problem to solve and a few minutes to decide who might solve it.

This file is meant to be edited. Change the settings first. Change the process or rules only when the output keeps missing the mark.

## Settings

| Setting | Default |
|---|---|
| Perspective | Third person. Never "I", "my" or "me". |
| Opening | Candidate's first name as the subject ("Maja is a senior data scientist..."). A name-less opening ("Senior data engineer with 9 years...") is fine when the name would feel repetitive. |
| Length | As long as it takes to sell, usually 3 to 5 sentences. No word cap. Cut any sentence that does not make the reader more interested. |
| Language | Match the language of the CV and the job description. |
| Tone | Confident, plain, specific. Simple words, short sentences, natural rhythm. |
| Audience | Hiring managers and recruiters for senior data science and IT roles. |
| Evidence | Framing and transferable skills are allowed (see Rules). |

## Process

1. **Read the job description.** Pull out the role title, the top 3 to 5 requirements and keywords, and the problem the hiring manager is really trying to solve.
2. **Choose one angle.** Decide the single strongest reason this candidate fits. Pick it from their most relevant experience, not simply their most recent or most impressive. Where there is a gap, bridge it with a transferable skill (see Rules).
3. **Write sentence 1: identity.** Name, a role title close to the job's own wording, years of experience, domain.
4. **Write sentences 2 and 3: proof.** The achievements, scale or outcomes that back up the angle. Use numbers when the CV has them.
5. **Write the last sentence: toolkit and value.** Key tools and methods from the job description, plus how the candidate works with clients and stakeholders. Consulting strengths matter here: getting up to speed fast, working with business teams, shipping to production.
6. **Cut and sharpen.** Remove filler, split long sentences, swap vague claims for specific ones. Run the Check below.

## Templates

Use these as starting shapes. Vary them so summaries for different candidates do not look identical.

**Experience-focused**
> [Name] is a [job title] with [X]+ years in [industry or domain]. Skilled in [strength], [strength] and [strength]. Known for [major impact], including [result]. [Name] brings [specific expertise] to [type of role or client problem].

**Skills and achievements-focused**
> [Name] is a [job title] with deep expertise in [skill A], [skill B] and [skill C]. [He/She/Name] [notable achievement], which [tangible outcome]. Now looking to apply [specialism] to [type of role or client objective].

## Rules

- **Keywords:** use the job description's own words for skills the candidate has or plausibly has ("machine learning", not "ML", if the job says so). Place them naturally, not as a list.
- **Transferable skills:** reframe real experience toward the target role. A/B testing becomes experimentation and product analytics. Pipeline work becomes data platform engineering. Leading a team becomes stakeholder and delivery experience. Reasonable inference is fine.
- **Hard limits:** never invent anything a recruiter can check and find false: employers, job titles, degrees, dates, or specific metrics and figures. Reframe, do not fabricate.
- **Years of experience:** count from the earliest relevant role. Write "N+" only when the dates support it, otherwise leave the number out.
- **Pronouns:** use "he" or "she" only when the CV makes it clear. Otherwise repeat the name or rewrite the sentence so no pronoun is needed. Do not guess.
- **No job description:** write a strong general summary for the candidate's main role.

## Avoid

These make the text sound machine-written or generic:

- Filler verbs and phrases: "leveraged", "spearheaded", "showcased", "demonstrated expertise in", "proven track record of", "passionate about", "stress the experience of".
- Adjective stacks: "results-driven, motivated, dynamic, self-starter".
- Meta commentary about the CV ("This summary highlights...").
- One long paragraph of generic claims that could describe anyone ("articulate communicator who speaks the languages of both people and technology"). It reads as vague, and the recruiter learns nothing.
- Sentences that need three commas to survive. Split them.

## Examples

> Ryan is an experienced developer with 8+ years building backend services and data platforms for fintech. He led the move of a payments platform to the cloud and mentors a team of five engineers. Strong in Python, Go and PostgreSQL.

> Maja is a senior data scientist who turns messy data into decisions. She has built forecasting and optimization models for e-commerce and ad tech, and led the teams that shipped them to production. Fluent in Python, SQL and BigQuery.

> Erik is a data scientist with a PhD in statistics and 6+ years in experimentation and optimization. He designed A/B tests that guided product launches and built pricing engines that sharpened demand forecasts. Comfortable in Python, BigQuery and AWS, and used to explaining results to people who do not read code.

> Maria is a senior data engineer with 9+ years building reliable data pipelines. Her real-time ingestion work carries straight over to the streaming use cases in this role. Hands-on with Kafka, Spark and cost-aware cloud design, and used to getting new client teams productive fast.

**Before** (first person, generic): "I am a motivated data scientist with a strong background in statistics. I am passionate about leveraging data to drive business results."

**After** (third person, specific, sells): "Erik is a data scientist with a PhD in statistics and hands-on experience in A/B testing, price optimization and click prediction. He builds models that improve demand forecasts. Strong in Python, BigQuery and AWS."

## Check

Before returning the summary, confirm:

1. Third person throughout, no "I" or "my".
2. The first sentence says who this is and why they fit the target role.
3. The job description's key keywords appear naturally.
4. Each sentence adds a reason to keep reading. Anything that does not is cut.
5. No banned filler words and no adjective stacks.
6. No invented employers, titles, degrees, dates or figures.
7. Read aloud, it sounds like a smart person talking to a busy hiring manager.
